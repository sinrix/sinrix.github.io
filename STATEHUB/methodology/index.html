What this version can tell you

Independent, nonpartisan presentation: all affiliations use the same probability definitions, seat scales and evidence standards. Neutral interface colors are separate from party identifiers. Reported inputs can favor either party; their values and the model’s assumptions remain inspectable. Independent and other-party members share the seat-data category “other”; coalition organization and officially nonpartisan chambers are labeled separately.

STATEHUB covers 99 chambers in 50 states and regular November 3, 2026 elections. Probabilities describe outcomes under a stated model. They remain experimental estimates: a high win probability is different from confidence in the model, and simulation precision cannot repair missing evidence.

The nationwide inputs now contain 5,730 district or ballot-position units accounting for all 6,139 regular-election seats in 88 election chambers, including Nebraska’s 24 seats modeled by reported partisan alignment. Eleven chambers have no regular election. Specials and intervening defections or vacancies remain outside this election scope.

MultiState supplies public district allocations, current terms and reported nominees. The latest composition review uses NCSL’s dated table, with earlier Open States records retained as context. 18 composition disagreements retain the prior published count with a confidence limit. One estimated holdover allocation remains. Candidate records are incomplete or pending for 33 seats. The 2026-10-20 review resolves 16 Massachusetts candidate-seat gaps using its official ballot list. Earlier official Illinois and Rhode Island lists resolved 269 gaps; those records and the prior editions are preserved. These are dated compilations rather than independently certified data from every state. Inspect all chambers and source conflicts in the evidence audit.

1. District baselines and candidates

The main explanatory baseline is the publisher’s allocation of 2024 presidential votes to the relevant 2026 district. Margins use D votes minus R votes divided by all presidential votes, including other candidates. Source, baseline year, map year, uncertainty and ballot structure are preserved. Publisher map matching has not been independently reproduced against every official boundary and precinct file.

Projected district margin = presidential baseline
+ national change × transfer + state adjustment
+ incumbency + candidate effect + trend + correlated errors

The new baselines use the official FEC national 2024 D−R margin of approximately −1.472 points as their national reference. They therefore receive 65% of the change between the current generic-ballot input and that presidential reference. Legacy inputs retain their own reference. This is a transparent normalization assumption, rather than an estimated historical transfer coefficient.

Complete single-seat D/R candidate pools receive a signed 1.5-point returning-incumbent advantage. With a candidate pool, the advantage enters candidate ranking once. Other local candidate effects and trends remain zero unless supplied. Complete same-party pools are fixed to that party; incomplete lists never establish an unopposed race. Possible write-in surprises beyond the reported roster are not estimated.

For bloc elections, candidates compete together for the actual number of seats; each candidate can win at most one seat. They share district swing and have individual ranking noise equal to district residual error divided by four. This noise is an uncalibrated prior. Separate ballot positions remain single-seat contests. Incomplete bloc pools use a shared party outcome for the unit, with an explicit warning.

Independent-only opposition uses an estimated presidential crosswalk with a 14-point residual error. When D/R and other candidates all run, other-candidate latent strength has center −30 and standard deviation 20. This broad prior preserves possible other-party outcomes but does not measure candidate support. These contests are limited to Medium-Low confidence. Ranking scores are not vote percentages; exports leave vote estimates unavailable for the new candidate-aware forecasts.

For a chamber with no usable district import, a documented fallback creates anonymous seat units from the current partisan share: q=(D contested seats + half contested vacancies + 0.5)/(partisan contested seats + 1), center=16×log(q/(1−q)), and logistic seat quantiles with spread 16. Other-party seats are assumed retained. This fallback does not identify geography or nominees and remains Low confidence. All current partisan election chambers have complete seat coverage.

2. Political environment

The current national input is 8.7 D−R points. The October 2 public Silver Bulletin update reports unadjusted D+9.0; its paragraph signature is September 30. The adjusted D+9.6 series is excluded. The source ledger distinguishes those dates from retrieval. No subscriber-only series is imported.

National error has standard deviation 3 points before its 65% transfer. State error is 3.5 points and is shared across both chambers. Missing state polling uses a zero-centered uncertain prior, while the observation remains unavailable. The scenario lab can change national/state margins, error and candidate/incumbency assumptions. Approval adjustment is hypothetical with a 0.25 transfer; no live approval series is imported.

3. Correlated Monte Carlo simulation

The baseline runs 50,000 trials per election chamber with deterministic seed 20260913. A trial shares national error across all chambers, state error across both state chambers, and Census-region error across states. Chamber and district residual errors add local uncertainty. National outcomes align the same trial indices.

National: Normal(0, 3²), transfer 0.65
State: Normal(0, 3.5²)
Region: Normal(0, 1.5²)
Chamber: Normal(0, 2²)
New district proxy residual: at least 10 points

Seeded streams and Box–Muller generate normal draws. Each trial adds contested winners to fixed holdovers and determines organization. Expected seats, medians, modes, histograms and 10th/90th percentiles come from the same trials. The 80% seat interval describes simulated seat outcomes; it is not a confidence interval for a win probability. Multi-seat district probabilities are average seat shares, and expected party seats sum to the unit’s magnitude.

At 50% with 50,000 trials, Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.22 percentage points. Input and model uncertainty are much larger. Browser scenarios use the published trial count and matched seed; unchanged controls return the saved baseline. Server scenarios use 25,000 trials, so comparisons may contain small sampling differences. Raising only Democratic swing with matched draws cannot decrease Democratic seat wins.

4. Institutions and organization

Nebraska: officially nonpartisan and unicameral, with one 49-seat Legislature and a 25-seat majority. Reported caucus/endorsement affiliations give 15 Democratic, 33 Republican and one independent current members; 9 Democrats and 16 Republicans are holdovers. The 24 general-election districts use reported candidate alignment while preserving NP ballot labels. Contests between same-alignment nominees fix the party outcome, not the individual winner. The forecast estimates underlying partisan majority, not formal partisan organization or every issue vote. The dated source records and alignment crosswalk are preserved in the source download.

Alaska: current coalitions are sourced separately from seat-party totals. A declared organization scenario allows up to 5 Republican senators or 2 Republican representatives to join all D/other members. If that group reaches a majority while Democrats alone do not, the organization outcome is coalition/other, with Democratic alignment because this declared coalition is built around all Democrats and other members. Its aligned control probability is displayed in blue and included with Democratic control; Republican-aligned coalitions use red under the same rule. Member dots retain actual affiliations. These crossover counts are uncalibrated assumptions about negotiations.

Holdovers: current seat holders with next election after 2026 provide reported splits, subject to seat-count and composition checks. Twenty-five of the 26 previously estimated partisan splits are replaced. Arkansas Senate retains an estimated split because the new roster’s vacancy count conflicts with the retained composition. State diagnostics test a one-seat holdover reassignment and valid dated composition alternatives. Alternatives inconsistent with fixed holdovers are disclosed as unavailable; the baseline does not silently reconcile them.

Ties: majority is floor(total/2)+1. Equal D/R seats without other members produce a tie; no executive tie-breaking rule is inferred. Without a party majority and with other members present, the outcome is other/unresolved. Current speaker organization is separately recorded. No-election chambers retain their dated organization and composition, conditional on no intervening changes.

Seat diagrams and uncertainty

Each dot represents one seat. The starting composition is the most frequent joint Democratic/Republican/other/vacant composition across all trials, with a representative trial retaining correlations between districts. It is not made by rounding expected party seats independently. Fixed holdovers retain their affiliations. A single-seat race with no affiliation reaching 55% is shown in neutral gray and included in the Tossup count, with its modal party visible in the tooltip. Other colors retain the joint modal composition, which can differ from a district-by-district list of favorites. The listed most likely composition includes those uncertain seats assigned to their modal outcomes; the colored summary leaves them unallocated.

For a multi-seat district, the kth modal party seat is assessed using simulated probabilities of winning at least k seats. It is neutral gray when no affiliation reaches 55% for that count, rather than treating average party seat share as an individual win probability. Dot locations are schematic, not actual floor seating or geographic district locations. Chamber control comes from full simulated majority/coalition outcomes, so uncertain seat dots and the chamber-control rating answer different questions.

5. External signals and double counting

Qualitative ratings remain comparison-only. Numeric expert forecasts and qualified markets are unavailable; their weights remain zero. Imported numeric signals are discounted for recency and deduplicated by independence group. Related records and common polling evidence require dependency review.

Market eligibility requires contract wording, settlement, liquidity, spread and relevance. Signal shifts are bounded to ±3 margin points, with expert weight at most 10%, market 5% and total 15%. These are design priors awaiting calibration. Signals enter the seat simulation rather than overwriting control probabilities afterward.

6. Quality, confidence and ratings

The 100-point rubric allocates composition 15, schedule 15, holdovers 10, district evidence 25, candidates 5, national polling 5, state polling 5, experts 5, markets 5, historical validation 5, and redistricting 5. Credit follows actual coverage and source freshness. Composition disagreement limits its component to 5; candidate credit follows the fraction with complete reported pools. Publisher map matching earns 3 of 5, and at least 30 eligible historical district cases earns only 2 of 5 validation points.

Quality is an explicit evidence rubric, rather than an accuracy score. Confidence also considers holdovers, observed district coverage and Monte Carlo stability. Estimated holdovers remain Low. Composition disagreements, incomplete nominees and complex candidate structures cap election confidence at Medium-Low. The full forecast remains uncalibrated, so no chamber receives High confidence. No-election confidence describes the dated fixed composition.

7. Historical evaluation

The new district diagnostic extracts 7,563 eligible single-seat races in 49 states and 88 chambers. Every presidential baseline predates the legislative election. Same-election or future baselines, uncertified results and multi-seat structures are excluded. Earlier-years fitting tests 7,450 races with at least two prior election years; each training year receives equal weight when choosing error from a fixed grid.

Nominal 80% margin coverage rises from 68.7% with the original 6-point error to 82.5% with earlier-years selections. Ten points is the most common selection and the all-history choice; it supplies the new proxy floor. The Brier difference’s state-cluster bootstrap interval includes zero, so an accuracy improvement is not established. Files were collected in 2026 with publisher-assigned historical maps. These are retrospective diagnostics with a neutral national environment, not forecast vintages or full chamber-control calibration.

The original 142 structural reconstructions across 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2024 remain unchanged. Separate temperature and seat-blending experiments retain their original archive and are not applied to this forecast. Their seated-composition target differs from certified election winners. Current polling transfer, shared errors, bloc elections, third-party strength and coalition negotiations still need validation. Inspect folds, matched comparisons, coverage limits and downloads.

8. Reproducibility and updates

Every publication retains its input hash, model version, parameters, seed, generation timestamp and source classifications. Earlier snapshots retain their actual dates; a new retrieval does not freshen the underlying source.

This edition serves a saved forecast and dated history. Scenarios run in a worker on your device using the same simulation model. They remain temporary. The data desk and audit provide downloads; reviewed updates are published by the owner.

Source extraction, eligibility rules, training cases, validation predictions and import scripts are preserved for reproduction. Changing candidate assumptions or model parameters requires a new version and a reviewed forecast publication.
